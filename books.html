Reading Log

Books

A running list of what I've read on data engineering, and what each one actually taught me.

Read

Fundamentals of Data Engineering

Joe Reis & Matt Housley

This is where the overall picture clicked for me — what data engineering actually is as a discipline, not just a stack of tools. It laid out the entire lifecycle end to end: generation, storage, ingestion, transformation, and serving, and how each stage connects to the next. It also gave me a sense of how the field evolved — from the Hadoop era to the modern cloud/lakehouse stack — and a mental map of the tools generally used at each stage, which made it much easier to place any new tool I ran into afterward.

Read

Spark: The Definitive Guide

Bill Chambers & Matei Zaharia

Where the first book gave me the map, this one took me under the hood of the engine I use daily. It gave me a much deeper understanding of how Spark actually functions and is built — the catalyst optimizer, how jobs get planned and executed, and why certain patterns (partitioning, joins, shuffles) behave the way they do. It shifted how I write Spark code, from "make it work" to reasoning about what the engine will actually do with it.

Currently reading

System Design Interview — An Insider's Guide, Volume 1

Alex Xu

Working through this one now to sharpen the system design side of things — how to think about scalability, trade-offs, and architecture decisions more deliberately, beyond what comes up naturally on the job.
